\section{Field demonstration}
\label{sec:7}

\subsection{Instrument and deployment}
\label{sec:7.1}

The field case is a thermal-dissociation broadband cavity-enhanced absorption spectrometer with two heated and one
ambient-temperature cavity, of the design described by \citet{Min_2016} and \citet{Nam_2022}. Ambient air is split between a
cell held at 180\,$^{\circ}$C, in which peroxy nitrates dissociate, and one at 300\,$^{\circ}$C, in which alkyl nitrates
dissociate as well \citep{Day_2002,Wooldridge_2010}; a third cell at ambient temperature measures NO$_2$ directly. The three differ in what has been
converted to NO$_2$ upstream rather than in how the spectrum is formed, and the sums of peroxy and alkyl nitrates
follow as channel differences, $\Sigma$ANs with the inter-channel factor $g'$ of Sect.~\ref{sec:4.4}. The retrieval
configuration of each channel -- window, baseline, references, shift and squeeze settings, wavelength calibration,
line shape, cavity constants and calibration schedule -- is given in Appendix~\ref{app:A}.

The instrument was deployed at Suncheon Shindae (SDD) from 2026-05-18 to 07-10 as part of a multi-institution
intensive campaign. Two properties belong to this deployment rather than to the configuration. The median fitted shift
in the 300\,$^{\circ}$C channel is \fieldnum{$-6.12$}\,px, inside its bound of $-10$\,px. The field numbers of this paper were checked
against a reprocessing with a corrected pressure-sensor pairing, which changes the channel NO$_2$ by $\pm$\fieldnum{0.25}\,\%, and are
reported as first computed. The instrument itself, its inlet and its conversion
efficiencies are characterised elsewhere.


\subsection{Noise floor of the product record}
\label{sec:7.2}

The noise floor of a channel-difference product has to be measured on the difference. The two hot channels share a
light source, a cavity and a clock, and their zero-air blocks coincide in time, so \fieldnum{155} blocks pair exactly and the
difference can be formed record by record (Table~\ref{tab:noise_floor}). The channels turn out to be nearly
uncorrelated, $r = \fieldnum{-0.093}$, so a quadrature assembly would have come within about \fieldnum{4}\,\% of the measured value; on an
instrument whose channels shared a drift it would not, and nothing in the fit report distinguishes the two cases.

The median at a true zero is \fieldnum{$+0.0054$}\,ppb, the same at the channel level and in the difference. A product assembled
through zero-air interpolation, a Rayleigh correction, a time-interpolated reflectivity, a spectral fit and a channel
subtraction therefore returns zero when the truth is zero, to a twentieth of its own detection limit. This closes the
additive chain end to end; a zero test is blind to gain errors, such as the scale discrepancy of Sect.~\ref{sec:3.6}.

The zero-air floor is roughly covered by the uncertainty column shipped with the product, at \fieldnum{0.84} to about \fieldnum{1.06}
times that column's \fieldnum{0.0806}\,ppb, depending on the scale. This does not conflict with Sect.~\ref{sec:4}: the zero-air
floor uses the same references minutes apart, whereas the structural budget measures what happens when a reference is
wrong. In \fieldnum{46.1}\,\% of the budget records the structural
change exceeds that record's own fit-propagated $\sigma$. The product column is \fieldnum{1.48} times the quadrature of the channel
columns, \fieldnum{0.0806}\,ppb against \fieldnum{0.0543}, because it is the inter-channel gain systematic of Appendix~\ref{app:D}, which scales
with the NO$_2$ amount; the ratios of Sect.~\ref{sec:4} are therefore taken against the fit-propagated sigma.

\subsection{The reporting convention in operation}
\label{sec:7.3}

Applied retrospectively to this deployment, the quantities of Sect.~\ref{sec:6.1} separate what the fit report catches from
what it does not, in the heated and ambient-temperature channels alike (Fig.~\ref{fig:6}); the provenance fields among
them are specified but not yet implemented. In Fig.~\ref{fig:6}f the residual flags an episode when the reduced $\chi^2$ exceeds 1.5, and the distance field when the
knot interval exceeds 2\,h or a knot departs by more than 50\,\% from its running median. The light level flags it when the
ambient or zero-air count falls below 30\,\% of its usual value; it was not assessed for the heated channels, and the cold
configuration, with fixed shift and squeeze, has no bound information in its termination state.

The fit report does not catch the displacement of Sect.~\ref{sec:5.4} at all. Across the deployment the reported
uncertainty of the ANs channel is a near-constant multiple of the residual RMS ($r = \fieldnum{+0.999}$ over all \fieldnum{74\,667} records;
the ratio varies by \fieldnum{2.8}\,\% between its 5th and 95th percentiles), so the two are one witness. Among the proposed fields,
the distance and in-range fields flag these records and only the provenance fields would name the cause.

The ambient-temperature channel supplies the opposite case. Its six excursions below $-5$\,ppb (\fieldnum{19} hourly medians between
19 May and 2 June) are all visible in the fit itself: the residual RMS is \fieldnum{8} to \fieldnum{600} times its campaign median and the
reduced chi-square \fieldnum{5.6} to \fieldnum{91}, against \fieldnum{1.2} in ordinary hours. Five of the six coincide with an abnormal reference
context: the bracketing reflectivity knots are \fieldnum{2.6} to \fieldnum{9.0}\,h apart against 1\,h nominal, or one of them departs from
its running median by \fieldnum{66} to \fieldnum{300}\,\% against a typical \fieldnum{10}\,\%. The distance and knot-validity fields therefore explain
them but are not needed to find them. The sixth, four hours on 2 June, has an ordinary reference context and is
identified by the residual alone. All six share a proximate cause that none of these fields names, the raw light level.
In each, the ambient or the zero-air spectrum, or both, fell below 30\,\% of its usual count at 460\,nm, in four of them to
within a few hundred counts of the detector dark level. On 2 June only the ambient path lost light (\fieldnum{22}\,\% of the
zero-air level), which is why the reference stayed ordinary. The misfit threshold is itself signal-dependent
(Sect.~\ref{sec:4.1}), and these excursions exceed it by an order of magnitude or more.

In the heated channels no hourly median falls below $-0.5$\,ppb over the deployment. The termination-state field flags
one episode: for eleven minutes on 21 June the 180\,$^{\circ}$C channel returned its shift at the $+5$\,px bound. The residual
is raised over the same records (reduced chi-square \fieldnum{4.7} to \fieldnum{5.5}), so the residual signals a problem and the
termination state identifies it. For the displacement and the bound episode, one of the context quantities supplies what the fit
report lacks; the light level and the heated-channel scale are beyond all of them (Sect.~\ref{sec:6.5}).
