\section{Verification}
\label{sec:3}

The verification covers five properties. The implementation solves the equations of Sect.~\ref{sec:2}, the analytic
Jacobian is the derivative it claims to be, and an independent implementation of the same problem returns the same
answer. The diagnostics of Sects.~\ref{sec:4} and \ref{sec:5} measure what they are said to measure on data whose
truth is known, and the two hot channels share a relative scale. Absolute concentration accuracy
against a reference standard is not established and is not claimed anywhere in this paper.

Results in Sects.~\ref{sec:3}--\ref{sec:6} are reported for three retrieval configurations of the field case of
Sect.~\ref{sec:7}, labelled by the cells they come from. Table~\ref{tab:1} lists what distinguishes them for the
retrieval: fitting window, baseline order, permitted shift range and light level; the full configuration is in
Appendix~\ref{app:A}. None of the results depends on the chemistry the cells measure, and where a result depends on a
property of a configuration, that property is named. Figure~\ref{fig:2} shows one record (1 June 2026, 10:35 KST; cold channel 10:34:34) retrieved in all three
configurations. Each window sits on the low-noise band set by its LED. The cold window extends 13\,nm below its LED
half-maximum, where the noise is several times higher. The step at 428\,nm in Fig.~\ref{fig:2}a is the band-pass filter
edge, and that at 470\,nm in Fig.~\ref{fig:2}b the red edge of the 469\,nm LED.

\subsection{Recovery of a known truth}
\label{sec:3.1}

The retrieval reproduces its own forward model to machine precision. Spectra are generated from the forward model of
Sect.~\ref{sec:2.1} with prescribed gas amounts, a Chebyshev baseline, a wavelength shift and a squeeze, and retrieved
with the same model. Over 27 combinations -- nine shifts from $-4$ to $+4$\,px (including sub-pixel values of
$\pm0.37$\,px) crossed with squeezes of 0.998, 1.000 and 1.002 -- the largest error in the retrieved NO$_2$ amount is
$9.5\times10^{-13}$ relative and the largest error in the recovered shift $6.7\times10^{-10}$\,px. This is a statement
about the solver, not about the instrument.

The baseline order is the one structural choice the retrieval makes on its own. Sweeping it from 0 to 8 on a
noise-free spectrum built with a fourth-order baseline, the retrieved NO$_2$ is in error by 27\,\% at order 0 and
0.75\,\% at order 1, and reaches machine precision at order 2 and above. Across the sweep the normalised condition
number rises from 14.9 to 33.8 and the degrees of freedom fall from 543 to 535. On noisy realisations of the same
spectrum the scatter of the retrieved NO$_2$ is 0.88 to 0.91 times the reported 1$\sigma$ at order 2 and above. For
this configuration, with white photon noise and a correct model, the reported uncertainty is therefore accurate to
about ten per cent, the baseline against which the structural terms of Sect.~\ref{sec:4} should be read
(Fig.~\ref{fig:3}a, b). Across the 13 synthetic instruments of Fig.~\ref{fig:1}a the white-noise ratio ranges from 0.91
to 1.32, within 10\,\% in 15 of the 30 cases. At nominal noise it lies between 1.22 and 1.32 in 9 of them, where the free wavelength shift adds scatter
that the covariance does not carry (Sect.~\ref{sec:3.4}); under autocorrelated noise it is 1.42 to 2.05.

\subsection{The analytic Jacobian}
\label{sec:3.2}

The two-term Golub--Pereyra derivative of Sect.~\ref{sec:2.3} agrees with a central finite difference of the
projected residual down to the round-off floor. The comparison is made inside a real fit: the optimiser call is
intercepted and the analytic Jacobian it was handed is differenced against the residual it was minimising. An
invariant check of this form runs on every fit in the test suite at the solver's default step; the step-size sweep
of Table~\ref{tab:jacobian_steps} turns that check into a measurement.

Both parameters give the expected V (Fig.~\ref{fig:3}c). The truncation branch falls with a fitted slope of
$+2.001$ in each, the $h^2$ of a central difference, and the round-off branch rises again as the difference of two
nearly equal numbers loses significance. The minima are $2.60\times10^{-9}$ for the shift and $2.64\times10^{-9}$ for
the squeeze, a ratio of 1.018. A floor common to both parameters is set by round-off in the shared residual
evaluation rather than by an error in either derivative. An expression that omitted a term, such as the one-term
approximation of \citet{Kaufman_1975}, would agree only down to the size of the omitted term and then flatten; the
descent to the floor identifies the expression as the derivative itself.

\subsection{An independent implementation of the same problem}
\label{sec:3.3}

QDOAS \citep{Danckaert_2017} solves the same separable least-squares problem by Marquardt--Levenberg with a
singular-value decomposition and a numerically differenced Jacobian. Because it does not model cavities, the
extinction spectrum is presented to it as a virtual transmission, $I = \exp(-K\alpha)$ with $I_0 = 1$, so that the
optical depth QDOAS forms is exactly $K\alpha$ and both codes solve the same equations on the same numbers.

On synthetic spectra the two codes agree to within the reported uncertainty. On noise-free spectra with shifts of
0, $-1.96$, $+2$ and $-4$\,px, Augur recovers NO$_2$ to $9.5\times10^{-13}$ relative and QDOAS to $5.0\times10^{-3}$,
the latter limited by its output resolution; QDOAS recovers the shift to within 0.0042\,px. On 30 noisy realisations
the two codes have root-mean-square relative errors of 0.0042 and 0.0045 against the same truth, and differ from
each other by at most $5.7\times10^{-3}$ relative in NO$_2$. In a larger test with 300 realisations in each of three
noise conditions (measurement noise, ten times that, and a narrow window with three times the noise), the standard deviation of the error normalised by the reported uncertainty is 0.97 to 1.05 for
both codes when they fit the same model. Started from zero, both recover true shifts between $-7$ and $+7$\,px with no
miss larger than a pixel (Fig.~\ref{fig:3}d, e). Switching on Augur's etalon terms in the narrow window
doubles the scatter there, which is a choice of model rather than a property of the solver.

On the field record the two codes agree closely in NO$_2$ but differ in scale. Over the 53 days, with each
spectrum's measured temperature and pressure used for the unit conversion and records selected by an
NO$_2$-conditional $\chi^2$ envelope, the NO$_2$ amounts agree with $r^2 \ge \fieldnum{0.990}$ in all three configurations
(Table~\ref{tab:qdoas_field}). The Deming slope QDOAS/Augur is \fieldnum{1.039} in the cold configuration and \fieldnum{1.063} and
\fieldnum{1.043} in the 180 and 300\,$^{\circ}$C configurations, where $r^2 = \fieldnum{1.000}$, so the heated-channel NO$_2$ scale
differs by \fieldnum{4}--\fieldnum{6}\,\%. The difference is partly tied to the wavelength shift. Within each day the QDOAS/Augur ratio rises with the fitted
shift by \fieldnum{0.9}\,\% per pixel in the 300\,$^{\circ}$C configuration (positive on \fieldnum{49} of \fieldnum{50} days; median fitted shift
\fieldnum{$-5.8$}\,px, both codes finding the same shift) and by \fieldnum{0.6}\,\% per pixel at 180\,$^{\circ}$C (\fieldnum{45} of \fieldnum{49} days). It does not
depend on the fitted stretch (Spearman $\rho = \fieldnum{-0.006}$). Augur shifts the pixel-domain reference, QDOAS the
wavelength-domain one through the calibration polynomial \citep{Beirle_2013}. The ambient-temperature configuration,
whose shift is fixed, still differs by \fieldnum{3.9}\,\%, so a common component of about \fieldnum{4}\,\% remains unidentified. The weak absorbers agree less well (CHOCHO $r^2$
\fieldnum{0.889}--\fieldnum{0.959}, slope \fieldnum{0.81}--\fieldnum{1.19}; H$_2$O slope \fieldnum{1.04}--\fieldnum{1.38}). Where the codes diverge most --
the wavelength shift in the cold window, and with it the weak absorbers there (Sect.~\ref{sec:5.1}) -- the data do not
determine the shift, so neither code's value is established by its fit.

\subsection{A benchmark that others can run}
\label{sec:3.4}

To make these measurements repeatable with other codes and cross sections we release a synthetic benchmark of 261
cases in six groups. They test exact recovery with no noise (A); reported uncertainty against realised scatter under
white, doubled and autocorrelated noise (B); bias from a deliberately incomplete model (C); cases whose wavelength
shift is not determined by the data (D); the same truth in two fitting windows (E); and the cost of the sub-pixel
interpolator against band-limited truth (F). Truth is known by construction; the spectra are built from the
instrument's own cross sections and wavelength calibration but contain no retrieval code, and the generator is
deterministic under a stated seed.

Group B shows that the reported uncertainty can fail for reasons that are calculable in advance. Augur returns
scatter-to-reported ratios of 1.32, 1.09 and 1.65 for white, doubled and autocorrelated noise, against 1.19, 1.33 and
1.57 from the reference implementation: both codes pass one condition of three, and they fail different ones. Fixing
the wavelength shift at its true value brings Augur's white-noise ratio to 1.19, so the difference between the codes lies
in the nonlinear degree of freedom and its search box, not in the propagated covariance. An AR(1) residual with
$\rho = 0.5$ inflates the variance of a sum by $(1+\rho)/(1-\rho) = 3$, so a covariance built on independence understates
the scatter by $\sqrt{3} = 1.73$. The measured 1.57 and 1.65 sit just below it, and fixing the shift moves Augur to 1.75,
because the free shift had absorbed part of the correlated structure (Fig.~\ref{fig:3}f). A sandwich covariance built
from the residual autocorrelation, $\mathbf{G}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r\mathbf{G}^{\top}$ with $\mathbf{G}$ the pseudo-inverse of the
design matrix and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r$ Toeplitz in the residual autocovariance up to its first non-positive lag, brings the
autocorrelated case to \fieldnum{1.04} (\fieldnum{1.08} with the shift fixed) and leaves the white-noise ratios at \fieldnum{1.31} and
\fieldnum{1.08}. The joint covariance of Eq.~(\ref{eq:9}) changes none of the ratios by more than \fieldnum{0.1}\,\%.

A deliberately plain profiled grid search is included as a reference implementation whose scorecard shows how a
submission is read. The benchmark does not rank codes: groups C and E are reported rather than passed or failed,
because no implementation is expected to cover what they measure.

\subsection{Relative scale between the two hot channels}
\label{sec:3.5}

The alkyl nitrate product is a difference between two channels, so their relative scale enters it directly. NO$_2$
was injected into both heated channels, with both ovens at their operating temperatures, at two concentration steps
on 2026-08-10/11. The steps did not equilibrate (the delivery line was still passivating), so only the ratio between
the channels is used. In the original processing the median of the per-record ratios was 1.228 at both steps. The median channel readings
were 14.75 and 11.97\,ppb, and 71.57 and 58.16\,ppb, whose ratios are 1.232 and 1.231. An origin regression over the 58
one-minute means of one file gives a slope of 0.817 with $r^2$ 0.9998, the reciprocal of the same laboratory ratio.
Reprocessed with the current code, block 2053 reads 1.239 times block 4101 (interquartile range 1.231--1.255, $n = 41$
records with block 4101 above 20\,ppb).

The relative scale is therefore constant over the concentration steps within this test. It is not constant between
calibration states (Table~\ref{tab:relscale}): the ratio differs by up to 30\,\% between them, and the campaign product
uses the value of each field state, $g' = \fieldnum{0.952}$ for 18 May--5 June, not the laboratory one.
 The test
also leaves the absolute recovery of either channel open and cannot separate optical from thermal causes of the
offset between the channels, because both cells were at their operating temperatures throughout.

\subsection{What is not verified here}
\label{sec:3.6}

A co-located reference analyser bounds what this paper can claim. Against the NIER air-quality-monitoring NO$_2$
analyser at the site (molybdenum-converter type), the operational NO$_2$ of the heated channels has
ordinary-least-squares gains of \fieldnum{0.57} (180\,$^{\circ}$C) and \fieldnum{0.65} (300\,$^{\circ}$C), while the ambient-temperature
channel has \fieldnum{0.92}--\fieldnum{1.00}. The median ratio of heated to ambient-temperature NO$_2$ is \fieldnum{0.72}, although the heated
channels measure NO$_2$ plus thermally produced NO$_2$ and should read at least as high. A laboratory injection with the
ovens at 300 and 180\,$^{\circ}$C gave a block-2053 sensitivity of at least 1.08 relative to the cylinder, so oven
temperature alone does not explain the discrepancy.

The discrepancy is not a time-base artefact. Before 29 May the heated channels correlate with the analyser at zero lag
($r = \fieldnum{0.985}$) and not at $\pm 9$\,h ($r \le \fieldnum{0.03}$), and their origin slopes are \fieldnum{0.61} (300\,$^{\circ}$C) and
\fieldnum{0.55} (180\,$^{\circ}$C) before 29 May and \fieldnum{0.58} and \fieldnum{0.49} after. It is present from the first full day (\fieldnum{0.59} on
20 May) and in every calibration state (median ratio to the analyser \fieldnum{0.59}, \fieldnum{0.55} and \fieldnum{0.50} in S1--S3), declining
somewhat later in the campaign, so it is not caused by a single intervention. Ozone-driven NO$_2$ loss in the ovens,
one of the known thermal-dissociation inlet interferences \citep{Thieser_2016,Dewald_2021,Wust_2025}, is not supported:
at night the 300\,$^{\circ}$C/ambient-temperature ratio rises with O$_3$ across O$_3$ terciles (\fieldnum{0.69}, \fieldnum{0.71}, \fieldnum{0.78}), and
the 180\,$^{\circ}$C channel reads lower than the 300\,$^{\circ}$C one. Factors common to the laboratory test and field operation --
the $R_L = 1$ assumption, the gas temperature recorded with each spectrum (median \fieldnum{31}--\fieldnum{33}\,$^{\circ}$C), the
cross-section temperature and the retrieval code, which QDOAS reproduces to \fieldnum{4}--\fieldnum{6}\,\% -- cannot produce a factor
of \fieldnum{0.6}, because the same processing gave at least 1.08 (300\,$^{\circ}$C) and 0.87 (180\,$^{\circ}$C) in the laboratory.
The laboratory standard entered through the inlet and passed the ovens, so the sampling path as operated there is
excluded as well. The remaining candidates are what differed between the two: the field calibration references
(reflectivity, helium and zero air), any plumbing used only at the site, the ambient matrix, and the reference analyser,
although the ambient-temperature agreement of \fieldnum{0.92}--\fieldnum{1.00} bounds a molybdenum-converter interference to a few
per cent. Separating them needs a standard added at the inlet during field operation and evaluated with the field
calibration references, and the cause remains unresolved. The error is
multiplicative and
leaves the residual, the reported uncertainty and every context field proposed in Sect.~\ref{sec:6} unchanged
(Sect.~\ref{sec:6.5}). No absolute accuracy is claimed. Sections~\ref{sec:3.1} and \ref{sec:3.3} verify the retrieval
against its own forward model and against another implementation of that model, which bounds implementation error
but not model error; model error is what the benchmark of Sect.~\ref{sec:3.4} measures.
