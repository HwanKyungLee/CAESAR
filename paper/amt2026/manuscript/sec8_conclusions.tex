\conclusions
\label{sec:8}

We have presented Augur, an open retrieval for a two-channel thermal-dissociation
broadband cavity-enhanced absorption spectrometer, posed in the extinction domain
as a separable nonlinear least-squares problem and solved by variable projection.
The formulation is not the contribution. The implementation makes three things
cheap: substituting a calibration reference and re-running the retrieval without
reprocessing the campaign, evaluating the projected objective anywhere in the
nonlinear parameters, and carrying to the product the state in which the optimiser
stopped and the distance to each calibration reference; recording each reference's
build time and time convention is specified here but not yet implemented. That turns
reliability questions from studies into routine measurements.

The code solves the problem it states: on noise-free synthetic spectra with
integer shifts the recovery is at the level of the linear algebra, the analytic
projected Jacobian agrees with finite differences, and Augur and QDOAS agree to
within the single-scan reported uncertainty -- where the fit can see. On the deployment the full chain returns \fieldnum{$+0.0054$}\,ppb
at a true zero, a twentieth of the product's three-sigma detection limit of
\fieldnum{0.203} to \fieldnum{0.243}\,ppb.

The uncertainty a fit reports is correct where its model is: with white noise and
a complete forward model the reported sigma tracks the synthetic scatter to within
about ten per cent. Against the calibration chain it does not. For the
channel-difference product, perturbing the zero-air reference, the reflectivity and
the etalon as the operational pipeline builds them gives a structural contribution of \fieldnum{1.40} (robust scale) to
\fieldnum{2.49} (standard deviation) times the reported sigma, and a total uncertainty
\fieldnum{1.72} to \fieldnum{2.68} times what the fit reports for the same 60\,s record. None
of it enlarges the residual. The magnitudes are this instrument's; the mechanism,
an intermittently measured reference interpolated to every record, is general.

A second class of shortfall needs no new measurement, because the pipeline already
holds the information. Every operational base fit
of the ambient-temperature channel returns its shift at the bound, and in one
heated channel \fieldnum{123} records terminate at a bound once the zero-air reference
is perturbed; all were recorded as converged. The rate depends on the search box
and step limit and is not comparable across implementations, but the state is
computed and then discarded. In one window the projected objective varies by about one per cent across four pixels of shift,
in another over five orders of magnitude, and both are reported as converged. And
a reflectivity reference on a superseded time convention displaced the alkyl
nitrates by \fieldnum{1.43} times the structural budget while moving the residual and
the reported uncertainty by two parts in a thousand.

We therefore propose that a retrieval report three numbers rather than one: the
fit uncertainty; the structural term for that record's own calibration context
rather than a campaign constant; and a context flag carrying the signed distance
to the nearest knot, the bracketing-interval length, an in-range flag, the number
of adjacent blocks the quality gates rejected, and each reference's identity,
build time and time convention. Every
implementation already computes these, so no change of algorithm is needed. Two
operational recommendations follow. Because the reflectivity interpolation error
depends only weakly on gap length, knot noise is reduced by averaging more scans per
calibration block, not by running blocks more often. And quality gates should be
assessed as variance-removal devices, not classifiers, and reported with the
coverage they cost.

The evidence is one instrument over one campaign. Two things should carry beyond
it: the reporting convention, which asks only for quantities every implementation
has, and the synthetic benchmark of 261 cases, which contains no retrieval code
and no field data and so favours no implementation -- though its cross sections
(convolved with this instrument's line shape) and windows make it independent of
the retrieval code, not of the instrument. Extending it across line shapes, windows
and pixel grids would separate what is general from what is ours; we release it so
that others can do so.

Two directions follow. The wavelength shift belongs to the spectrograph, not the
air, so fitting one shift jointly across the spectra of a calibration block -- a separable problem with common nonlinear parameters -- should
remove the indeterminacy this paper only diagnoses; a laboratory spectrum with a
few ppb of added NO$_2$ already locates the ambient-temperature channel's shift to
a tenth of a pixel (Sect.~\ref{sec:5.1}), so existing calibration blocks would serve
if the absorber were supplied. And treating the zero-air reference and the
reflectivity as measured quantities with their own covariance, not exact inputs,
would propagate the structural term inside the fit rather than afterwards.

Third, at two interventions during the deployment the helium/zero-air contrast
changed by about \fieldnum{5} and \fieldnum{20}\,\% while ambient absorption did not; only
the helium-filled intensity moved, a multiplicative error the fit report cannot
see. Carrying the helium and zero-air intensities of each calibration block,
not only their ratio, and the raw light level of each spectrum -- which alone
accounts for all six ambient-temperature excursions of Sect.~\ref{sec:7.3} -- would
show such steps where they happen instead of leaving them to be inferred from the
product.
