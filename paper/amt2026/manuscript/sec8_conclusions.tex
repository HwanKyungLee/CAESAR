\conclusions
\label{sec:8}

We have presented Augur, an open retrieval posed in the extinction domain as a separable nonlinear least-squares
problem and solved by variable projection, configured by instrument and campaign profiles rather than written for one
instrument, and demonstrated its diagnostics on one field case: a thermal-dissociation broadband cavity-enhanced
absorption spectrometer with two heated and one ambient-temperature cavity.
The formulation is not the contribution. The implementation makes three things
cheap: substituting a calibration reference and re-running the retrieval without
reprocessing the campaign, evaluating the projected objective anywhere in the
nonlinear parameters, and carrying to the product the state in which the optimiser
stopped and the distance to each calibration reference; recording each reference's
build time and time convention is specified here but not yet implemented. That turns
reliability questions from studies into routine measurements.

The code solves the problem it states: on noise-free synthetic spectra with
integer shifts the recovery is at the level of the linear algebra, the analytic
projected Jacobian agrees with finite differences, and Augur and QDOAS agree to
within the single-scan reported uncertainty; on field spectra the two codes agree in NO$_2$ with
$r^2 \ge \fieldnum{0.990}$ but differ in the heated-channel scale by \fieldnum{4}--\fieldnum{6}\,\%. On the deployment the full chain returns
\fieldnum{$+0.0054$}\,ppb at a true zero, a twentieth of the product's three-sigma detection limit of
\fieldnum{0.203} to about \fieldnum{0.257}\,ppb -- a test of the additive chain only.

The uncertainty a fit reports is correct where its model is: with white noise,
a complete forward model and a well-identified shift the reported sigma tracks the synthetic scatter to within
about ten per cent, and with a free shift the ratio reaches 1.32. Against the calibration chain it does not. For the
channel-difference product, perturbing the zero-air reference, the reflectivity and
the etalon as the operational pipeline builds them gives, in the best-calibrated week, a structural contribution of \fieldnum{1.28} (robust scale) to
\fieldnum{2.29} (standard deviation) times the reported sigma, and a total uncertainty
\fieldnum{1.63} to \fieldnum{2.50} times what the fit reports for the same 60\,s records. None
of the calibration-chain terms enlarges the residual. The magnitudes are this instrument's; the mechanism,
an intermittently measured reference interpolated to every record, is general.

A second class of shortfall needs no new measurement, because the pipeline already
holds the information. With the cold shift bounded to $\pm 1$\,px in a sensitivity run, every base fit
of the ambient-temperature channel returned its shift at the bound, and in the budget propagation
\fieldnum{187} of \fieldnum{9034} records were excluded because a fit, unperturbed or perturbed, terminated at a bound; all were
recorded as converged. The rate depends on the step limit (Fig.~\ref{fig:1}b) and
differs between implementations (Sect.~\ref{sec:5.1}), but the state is computed and then discarded. In one window the projected objective varies by about one per cent across four pixels of shift,
in another over five orders of magnitude, and both are reported as converged. And
a reflectivity reference on a superseded time convention displaced the alkyl
nitrates by \fieldnum{1.44} times the structural budget while moving the residual and
the reported uncertainty by two parts in a thousand. Neither the fit report nor the proposed fields see the
largest error in this record (Sect.~\ref{sec:6.5}): against a co-located NIER analyser the heated-channel NO$_2$ reads low by a
factor of about \fieldnum{0.6} while the ambient-temperature channel agrees, a multiplicative discrepancy whose cause is
unresolved (Sect.~\ref{sec:3.6}).

We therefore propose that a retrieval report three numbers rather than one: the
fit uncertainty; the structural term for that record's own calibration context
rather than a campaign constant; and a context flag carrying the distance
to the nearest knot and its side, the bracketing-interval length, an in-range flag, the number
of adjacent blocks the quality gates rejected, and each reference's identity,
build time and time convention. Every
implementation already computes all of these except the build time and time convention of a reference,
which must be recorded when the reference is built; no change of algorithm is needed. Two
operational points follow. Where the interpolation error is set by knot noise, as in the 180\,$^{\circ}$C channel,
averaging more scans per calibration block rather than running blocks more often is the lever to test; the
within-block evidence of Sect.~\ref{sec:6.2} is unfavourable and the decisive measurement has not been made. And quality gates should be
reported with the coverage they cost, because they remove coverage as well as variance (Sect.~\ref{sec:6.4}).

The evidence is one field case, one instrument over one campaign. The code is not tied to that instrument
(Appendix~\ref{app:A}), but its behaviour on other configurations is not shown here. Two things should carry beyond
it: the reporting convention, which asks for quantities every implementation
has or can record when a reference is built, and the synthetic benchmark of 261 cases, which contains no retrieval code
and no field data and so favours no implementation -- though its cross sections
(convolved with this instrument's line shape) and windows make it independent of
the retrieval code, not of the instrument. Extending it across line shapes, windows
and pixel grids would separate what is general from what is ours; we release it so
that others can do so.

Three directions follow. The wavelength shift belongs to the spectrograph, not the
air, so fitting one shift jointly across the spectra of a calibration block -- a separable problem with common nonlinear parameters -- could
remove the indeterminacy this paper only diagnoses (Sect.~\ref{sec:5.1}); a laboratory spectrum with a
few ppb of added NO$_2$ already locates the ambient-temperature channel's shift to
a tenth of a pixel (Sect.~\ref{sec:5.1}), so existing calibration blocks would serve
if the absorber were supplied. And treating the zero-air reference and the
reflectivity as measured quantities with their own covariance, not exact inputs,
would propagate the structural term inside the fit rather than afterwards (Sect.~\ref{sec:4.6}).

Third, the raw light level of each spectrum -- the proximate cause of all six ambient-temperature
excursions of Sect.~\ref{sec:7.3} -- and the helium and zero-air intensities of each calibration block, not only
their ratio, should travel with the product (Sect.~\ref{sec:6.5}), so that changes in the light reaching the detector
can be seen where they happen instead of being inferred afterwards from the product.
