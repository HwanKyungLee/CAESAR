\introduction
\label{sec:1}

A DOAS retrieval returns more than a concentration. It returns a residual, a
chi-square, a parameter covariance and a convergence flag, and these travel
with the concentration into averaging, into filtering, into trend fits and
into comparisons with models and with other instruments. In practice they are
read as a statement about how much the retrieval can be trusted: a small
residual means the model described the spectrum, a converged flag means the
optimiser found the answer, and the reported 1$\sigma$ means the concentration
is known that well. Each of these readings is conditional, and the conditions
are not reported alongside the numbers.

That the conditions matter is not a new observation. The uncertainty a DOAS
fit reports is the propagation of the residual scatter through the linear
system, and it is correct only if the residual is white and the forward model
is complete; \citet{Stutz_1996} showed that structure in the residual
inflates the true error well beyond the reported one, and the standard
treatment \citep{Platt} sets out the conditions explicitly.
Practitioners respond with rules of thumb -- multiply the reported error by
two, or by the ratio of the residual to the photon noise -- and with
sensitivity studies on the spectral fit: the choice of window, the polynomial
order, the set of cross sections included.

What that literature examines is the spectral fit. The quantities the fit
treats as given -- the reference spectrum, the mirror reflectivity, the
instrument line shape -- are usually characterised separately, assigned a
calibration uncertainty, and combined with the fit uncertainty at the end. For
a cavity-enhanced instrument this is a stronger assumption than it appears.
The reference and the reflectivity are not constants with an uncertainty; they
are time series, measured intermittently and interpolated to every
measurement, and the error they contribute to a given record depends on where
that record sits relative to the nearest calibration. A campaign-mean
calibration uncertainty, of the kind reported for the effective path length in
earlier instruments of the design used here \citep{Min_2016,Nam_2022}, does not
describe it.

Intercomparison campaigns approach the question from the other side. By
running many implementations on the same spectra -- as CINDI-2 \citep{Kreher_2020} and its predecessors have done for MAX-DOAS -- they measure the spread between codes,
which bounds implementation error and has been decisive in standardising
settings. But a spread is not an error: when implementations agree they may
agree on a common mistake, and when they disagree the comparison cannot say
which is right, because there is no truth. Synthetic studies supply the truth
that intercomparisons lack, and are the standard way to verify a solver, but
they are normally built to test the fit -- given a spectrum, is the
concentration recovered -- and so inherit the same boundary: the references
are correct by construction.

This paper takes the boundary as the subject. Treating the calibration chain
as part of the retrieval rather than as its input divides the ways a fit
report overstates its knowledge into two kinds, which want different remedies.
The first is what the fit cannot know: contributions that leave the residual
unchanged, so that no amount of care in reading the fit report will reveal
them. These have to be measured by perturbing the retrieval and re-running it,
and added to the budget. The second is what the pipeline already knows and
does not carry to the product: whether the optimiser stopped at a bound,
whether the nearest reflectivity measurement was fifteen minutes or eleven
hours away, whether a quality gate upstream rejected the calibration blocks on
either side. This information exists inside the software at the moment the
concentration is computed and is discarded between one step and the next. It
costs nothing to keep, and keeping it turns a silent failure into a flagged
one.

We present Augur, an open retrieval built so that both kinds of question can be asked cheaply enough to ask
routinely. The retrieval is posed in the extinction domain as a separable nonlinear
least-squares problem and solved by variable projection. It is not written for one instrument: the layout of the
raw data, the calibration flags and the fit configuration are read from instrument and campaign profiles
(Appendix~\ref{app:A}), and the same code runs other configurations through such profiles. The diagnostics are
demonstrated here on one field case, a thermal-dissociation broadband cavity-enhanced absorption spectrometer
with two heated and one ambient-temperature cavity measuring NO$_2$, total peroxy nitrates and total alkyl
nitrates over 53 days in 2026; other configurations are not evaluated in this paper. We verify it against
synthetic truth and against an independent DOAS implementation, measure the
structural terms it cannot report -- which together exceed the sigma it
reports by a factor of \fieldnum{1.6} to \fieldnum{2.5} in the best-calibrated week -- map the identifiability of its
nonlinear parameters, and describe twelve days of
operational product built from a calibration reference that a later correction
had made stale, an error \fieldnum{1.44} times that budget which changed the residual and the reported
uncertainty by two parts in a thousand while moving the 300\,$^{\circ}$C channel's concentration by two
reported standard deviations. We
propose a small reporting convention whose distance fields would have flagged it at the first
reprocessing and whose provenance fields, once implemented, would name its cause,
and release a synthetic benchmark with which any DOAS implementation can
measure the same quantities on the same cases.

The evidence is one field case -- one instrument over one campaign -- which bounds what can be
concluded about instruments in general. Two things are intended to carry
beyond it. The benchmark of Sect.~\ref{sec:3.4} is built from cross sections and a wavelength
calibration but contains no retrieval code and no field data, so the measurements it supports do not depend on any retrieval implementation, Augur included; its cross sections, fitting windows and wavelength grid are those of one instrument, and carrying it to another requires that instrument's own. And
the reporting convention of Sect.~\ref{sec:6} asks for quantities that every implementation
already computes, apart from the build time and time convention of each reference, which must be recorded when the
reference is built; adopting it requires no change of algorithm. Where a
result does depend on this instrument -- the magnitude of the reflectivity
term, the behaviour of the cold channel -- we say so.
