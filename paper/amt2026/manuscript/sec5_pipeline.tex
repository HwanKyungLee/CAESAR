\section{What the pipeline already knows and does not report}
\label{sec:5}

Section~\ref{sec:4} measured what the fit cannot know: errors that enter $\alpha$ through
inputs the fit never sees, and which therefore leave no trace in the residual
it minimises. This section is about the other category, and it is the cheaper
one to fix. In each case below, the information needed to qualify a retrieved
amount already exists somewhere in the pipeline -- in the profiled objective, in
the optimiser's termination state, in the knot table, in the timestamps of the
files being read -- and is discarded before the product is written. Nothing here
calls for a better retrieval algorithm or an additional measurement. It calls
for the pipeline to report what it has already computed.

\subsection{A nonlinear parameter the data do not determine}
\label{sec:5.1}

A nonlinear least-squares routine reports convergence when its step and
tolerance criteria are met. That is a statement about the optimiser, not about
the objective: an iteration that stops because the step became small has
established that it is at a stationary point of the profiled residual, and
nothing more. If the profiled residual is nearly flat over the whole range the
parameter is allowed to take, the returned value is set by where the search
started and where the bounds are, and the uncertainty computed from the
curvature at that point describes a curvature that is not there.

This is not a defect of any particular implementation. It follows from
reporting a parameter value without reporting how much the data constrain it,
which is standard practice in DOAS retrieval software.

\strong{A measured case: the wavelength shift in the cold channel.} The wavelength
shift is the natural test because it is the parameter the retrieval is least
able to distinguish from a change in the background. Scanning the shift across
$\pm 4$\,px and profiling out the linear parameters at each point gives the
objective's actual dependence on it.

In the cold channel the objective is flat. Across the whole $\pm 4$\,px range
the ratio of the largest to the smallest profiled residual sum of squares has
a median of \fieldnum{1.0120} and never exceeds \fieldnum{1.0611}; in a set of \fieldnum{165} spectra the
minimum lies at an edge of the scanned range in \fieldnum{77}\,\% of cases, and evaluating
the objective at zero shift rather than at its minimum costs between \fieldnum{0.01}\,\%
and \fieldnum{4.35}\,\%. The same scan in the hot PNs channel spans a factor of
$\fieldnum{3.09\times10^{5}}$.

Two implementations confirm that the consequence is not numerical. Under
matched settings the shift returned for the same spectra is \fieldnum{$-2.014$}\,px in
this work, with \fieldnum{85.5}\,\% of fits terminating at a bound, against \fieldnum{$-0.088$}\,px
from QDOAS with \fieldnum{4.5}\,\% at a bound; tightening the QDOAS convergence threshold
to $10^{-12}$ changes nothing. Both codes converge, both report a value, and
the values differ by two pixels because the data do not distinguish them.

The same channel does distinguish them when the absorber is strong enough. On
2 June NO$_2$ from a cylinder standard (nominally 10\,$\mu$mol\,mol$^{-1}$ in N$_2$, past its
certification date and used here only for its spectrum) was added to zero air
in the cold cavity for half an hour; retrieved against the zero-air spectrum that preceded it, with the
operational reference and window, the plateaus at 9.3 and 3.6\,ppb place the
minimum of the objective at $-0.10$ and $-0.15$\,px, and evaluating it at
$-2.0$\,px instead costs 39\,\% and 7.5\,\% of the residual, against nothing at all
for the zero-air spectrum itself. The QDOAS value lies within 0.06\,px of that
minimum; the value returned here does not, and the operational fixed value of
$-0.5$\,px costs 2.0\,\% at 9.3\,ppb. What the ambient spectra lack is not a defined
shift but the column to locate it (Fig.~\ref{fig:5}b, c).

What the retrieved amounts do with that freedom is the point. Regressing the
cold-channel retrievals against the hot-channel reference over \fieldnum{5364} spectra,
allowing the shift to float gives slopes of \fieldnum{0.940} (NO$_2$), \fieldnum{0.858} (CHOCHO) and
\fieldnum{1.027} (H$_2$O); fixing the shift at $-0.5$\,px gives \fieldnum{1.019}, \fieldnum{0.662} and \fieldnum{1.178}.
The weakest absorber in the window moves by \fieldnum{23}\,\% according to a parameter the
residual cannot resolve.

On the ambient spectra alone the external check does not settle which
treatment is right, and that is the substance of the problem rather than a gap
in it. For the two species that do
not dissociate in the heated cell, and whose slope against the reference
should therefore be unity, the fitted shift agrees better -- \fieldnum{0.858} against
\fieldnum{0.662} for CHOCHO, \fieldnum{1.027} against \fieldnum{1.178} for water; for NO$_2$ the fixed shift
does. A convention cannot be chosen on this evidence, and choosing one
silently is what current practice does. The laboratory spectrum answers what the
ambient ones cannot: the shift this channel supports is close to zero,
$-0.10$\,px, near neither the floated \fieldnum{$-2.0$}\,px nor the fixed $-0.5$\,px. Neither
treatment was right, and neither fit report could have said so. What locates
the parameter is a spectrum with enough absorber; what the product should carry
is the value such a spectrum gives, and where it came from.

\strong{What is degenerate with what.} Not every input error threatens the same
parameter. Propagating the three structural terms of Sect.~\ref{sec:4} separately and
recording how far each moves the wavelength shift separates them cleanly
(Table~\ref{tab:shift_degeneracy}).

An error in the reflectivity changes the magnitude of the extinction and
essentially nothing else -- it never moves the shift by a tenth of a pixel. An
error in the zero-air reference, or in the etalon frequency, changes the shape
of the extinction and is therefore partly absorbed by the axis. This is the
same distinction that Sect.~\ref{sec:6.1a} draws from the shape statistic, arrived at
independently: the reflectivity is a pure amplitude term, and the other two
compete with the nonlinear parameters.

It also bounds the concern that the budget of Sect.~\ref{sec:4} is really measuring basin
hopping. Records whose shift moved by more than a pixel under perturbation are
\fieldnum{6.2}\,\% of the total and carry \fieldnum{4.8}\,\% of the variance, and removing them raises
the budget rather than lowering it. The large-shift records are
the low-amplification ones, where the absorption is deep and the retrieval is
well conditioned.

\strong{What the pipeline could report instead.} The profiled scan above is too
expensive to run per spectrum, but its conclusion is available cheaply from the
ratio of the objective at the returned value to the objective at a fixed
reference point -- a quantity the fit evaluates anyway. Where that ratio lies
within the noise, the parameter should be fixed, said to be fixed, and the cost
of fixing it carried in the uncertainty of every amount that depends on it.
Reporting \fieldnum{$-2.014 \pm 0.03$}\,px for a quantity the data locate only to \fieldnum{$\pm 2$}\,px
is the failure this subsection describes, and for this channel the resulting
term is larger than any in Table~\ref{tab:2}.

\subsection{Termination at a bound is a routine outcome}
\label{sec:5.2}

Bound termination is usually treated as a failure to be logged. In this
instrument it is the normal state of one channel and a systematic response to
perturbation in another.

The operational configuration allows the shift different freedom in each
channel -- $[-10, +0.5]$\,px in hot ANs, $[-5, +5]$\,px in hot PNs, $[-1, +1]$\,px
in cold -- and in the cold channel every one of the \fieldnum{22} base fits returns
exactly \fieldnum{$-1.000$}\,px, the bound itself. The fits are reported as converged. In
the production propagation of Sect.~\ref{sec:4} the cold shift is held fixed rather than
bounded, and moves by exactly zero in every record, which confirms the
configuration but also means the channel contributes no information about the
parameter at all.

In hot PNs, \fieldnum{123} records terminate at a bound under perturbation, and \fieldnum{121} of
those are the zero-air perturbation; only \fieldnum{4} are unperturbed fits. The hot ANs
channel has none. The operational fit is therefore well inside its feasible
region, and it is the structural perturbation that drives it out -- which is to
say that the margin between the operational solution and the edge of
identifiability is smaller than the size of a reference error. This is also
the ground on which Sect.~\ref{sec:4} excludes bound-terminated records from Table~\ref{tab:2}: a fit
pinned at a bound has not converged, and its response is not a measurement of
anything.

\strong{What the pipeline could report instead.} The optimiser distinguishes a fit
stopped by the gradient from one stopped by a step limit or at a parameter
bound, and Augur computes these as distinct states internally. In the delivered campaign product they were not
carried alongside the retrieved amount; the current version exports them as
separate columns (Sect.~\ref{sec:2.6}). Collapsing
them into ``converged'' is what allows a bound-pinned shift to be published as a
measurement.

\subsection{How far a record is from its calibration}
\label{sec:5.3}

Every retrieved amount sits at some distance in time from the calibration
knots that produced its reference spectra, and that distance varies over the
campaign by more than an order of magnitude. The pipeline computes it -- it has
to, in order to interpolate -- and then discards it. The retrieval interpolates
the reflectivity across an \fieldnum{11.8}\,h gap exactly as it does across a one-hour gap,
and returns the same uncertainty in both cases.

Coverage was measured over the whole campaign by extracting the calibration
blocks directly from the raw files, independently of the retrieval, and
verified against the production knot cache (\fieldnum{157}/\fieldnum{157} hot and \fieldnum{164}/\fieldnum{164} cold knots
at 0.000000\,s) and against the alpha header's own \texttt{ZA\_count=}\fieldnum{1313}. Two
distinct deficits appear, and they are not the same quantity (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}).

For the zero-air reference the word ``interpolation'' is accurate: \fieldnum{0.08}\,\% of hot
records lie outside the knot range and \fieldnum{0.07}\,\% inside a gap longer than two
hours, with the nearest knot a median \fieldnum{922}\,s away. The zero-air knot set is on
the corrected time axis -- the production cache over 05-18..24 reproduces a
re-extraction at 0.000000\,s -- so the $I_0$ column is unaffected by the
displacement described in Sect.~\ref{sec:5.4}.

The cold channel's deficit is real and is a gap problem: the existing quality
gates reject \fieldnum{11.3}\,\% of cold pairs against \fieldnum{0.24}\,\% of hot pairs (\fieldnum{3} of \fieldnum{1\,264});
an independent count gives \fieldnum{75} of \fieldnum{743}, \fieldnum{10.1}\,\%, and nothing below turns on the
difference. Independent rejection at the \fieldnum{11.3}\,\% rate would leave \fieldnum{3.5}\,\% of cold
records in gaps longer than two hours against the \fieldnum{11.03}\,\% observed, and the
worst gap, \fieldnum{11.8}\,h, is eleven consecutive rejected pairs -- a run that even at a
40\,\% independent rejection rate would be expected 0.03 times in the campaign.
The rejections arrive in runs, which is the episodic structure Sect.~\ref{sec:6.1a} found in
the reflectivity variance itself, seen from the calibration side.

The two deficits also explain a limit on Table~\ref{tab:2} itself. A perturbation
experiment can only be run on scans that have neighbouring knots of both
reference types, so periods in which the calibration failed are absent from the
budget population -- and those are precisely the periods with the largest
errors. In the cold channel, eleven of the thirteen zero-air blocks identified
as defective in Sect.~\ref{sec:6} fall outside the case set because no reflectivity knot
exists within \fieldnum{2.8}--\fieldnum{5.8}\,h of them: a collapse of the zero-air signal takes the
He/zero-air pair down with it, so the two reference failures are one event
seen twice.

\strong{What the pipeline could report instead.} Three numbers the interpolation
already holds: the elapsed time to the nearest knot of each reference type, the
length of the enclosing knot interval, and a flag for records outside the knot
range altogether rather than between knots. Section~\ref{sec:6.1} specifies them as
product fields. They cost nothing to emit and they separate the \fieldnum{0.00}\,\% of the
budget window from the \fieldnum{18.31}\,\% of the cold window, which no field in the
current product distinguishes.

\subsection{A reference error the fit does not see}
\label{sec:5.4}

Table~\ref{tab:2} is computed on a clock-aligned reflectivity reference. The operational
one was not aligned: the reflectivity knots for 2026-05-18 to 05-30 lie on a
time axis that a later correction to the raw-data reader superseded, while the
spectra lie on the corrected one, so every record in that span was retrieved
with a reflectivity displaced by nine hours. The budget window of Sect.~\ref{sec:4} falls
inside it. The zero-air reference is unaffected -- its knots were already on
the corrected axis, verified to 0.000000\,s -- and so is the cold channel, which
the correction does not apply to.

This makes it possible to measure directly what using a wrong reference costs,
and what the fit report says about it. The retrieval was run over all \fieldnum{9\,132}
records of the window twice, changing nothing but the knot time base
(Table~\ref{tab:clock_displacement}).

The extinction spectrum changes by an amount comparable to the single-scan
noise floor of $\fieldnum{6.16\times10^{-9}}$. The residual and the reported uncertainty
change by the same two parts in a thousand, and the agreement is not a
coincidence: across the campaign the reported uncertainty is a near-constant
multiple of the residual RMS ($r = \fieldnum{+0.999}$; the ratio moves by \fieldnum{2.8}\,\% between
its 5th and 95th percentiles, Sect.~\ref{sec:7.3}). A fit report that shows an unchanged
residual and an unchanged uncertainty is not offering two independent
witnesses to its own quality. It is offering one, twice. The fitted wavelength shift moves by
\fieldnum{0.005}\,px, where the reference perturbations of Table~\ref{tab:2} move it by \fieldnum{0.23}\,px --
forty times less -- so the error is neither absorbed by the nonlinear
parameters nor revealed by the residual. The retrieved concentration moves by
two reported standard deviations. Every quantity a fit report offers as
evidence of quality is unchanged; the answer is not.

Correcting it did not simply reduce the budget, and the way it did not is
informative. The robust scale of the $\Sigma$ANs budget fell by \fieldnum{7.5}\,\%
(\fieldnum{0.0758} to \fieldnum{0.0701}\,ppb) while its standard deviation rose by \fieldnum{5.2}\,\% (\fieldnum{0.1184} to
\fieldnum{0.1245}), so the total uncertainty relative to what is reported widened at both
ends, from \fieldnum{1.81}--\fieldnum{2.57} to \fieldnum{1.72}--\fieldnum{2.68} times against the sigma the fit reports
(Sect.~\ref{sec:4.4}). Two things were working in opposite
directions. Coverage improved, from \fieldnum{93.0}\,\% to \fieldnum{98.7}\,\% of the window's records,
because the misaligned knots had been falling outside the one-hour window that
admits a record to the perturbation experiment. And the correlation between
the two channels' joint responses fell from \fieldnum{$+0.412$} to \fieldnum{$+0.146$}: because
$\Sigma$ANs is a channel difference, a common-mode error partly cancels in it,
and the pre-correction cancellation was an artefact of an error the two
channels shared. The contaminated product was flattering itself in two ways at
once, and the budget measured on it inherited both.

The cost is not uniform in time, and not in the direction we predicted before
measuring it. Across the boundary where the clock was corrected, the affected
side gives \fieldnum{0.0600}\,ppb against \fieldnum{0.0000}\,ppb on the unaffected side -- the latter a
check that the realignment touched only the intended knots -- so the
displacement costs less near the boundary than in the budget window. What
matters is not proximity to the boundary but how fast the reflectivity is
moving at the time, and it moves fastest early in the deployment. The
budget-window figure of \fieldnum{0.0999}\,ppb is the value to quote; \fieldnum{0.0600}\,ppb is range
information, not a worst case.

\strong{What the pipeline could report instead.} The failure here is not that the
clock was wrong -- that was a hardware fact, caught by an external reference and
corrected. It is that a derived cache went stale and nothing noticed. The
reflectivity file was written on 2026-07-10; the correction to the reader that
invalidated it was committed on 2026-09-19; the retrieval read both without
comparing them. A pipeline that records, for each derived reference, the
version of the code and the raw files it was built from can detect this
condition mechanically, and it is the only failure in this paper that admits a
complete remedy rather than a better report. Section~\ref{sec:6.1} specifies the fields.
