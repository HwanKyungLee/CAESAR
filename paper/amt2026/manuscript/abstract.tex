\begin{abstract}
A DOAS fit reports a residual, a chi-square, an uncertainty and a convergence
flag, and these are read as a description of how well the retrieval did. Each
can assert more than the fit knows, and the gap is invisible from inside the
fit report. We present Augur, an open retrieval for a two-channel
thermal-dissociation broadband cavity-enhanced absorption spectrometer
measuring NO$_2$, total peroxy nitrates and total alkyl nitrates
\citep{Min_2016,Nam_2022}, and use it to measure that gap on 53 days of field
measurements.

Augur poses the retrieval in the extinction domain as a separable nonlinear
least-squares problem and solves it by variable projection with an analytic
two-term Golub--Pereyra Jacobian, propagating a joint covariance that includes
the nonlinear parameters. Its contribution, however, is architectural rather
than numerical. The time-resolved references enter as data, so a
leave-one-out perturbation of the calibration record is a loop over knots
rather than a reprocessing campaign; and the projected objective is available
as a function of the nonlinear parameters, so the shape of the minimum can be
mapped rather than inferred from a converged point. The retrieval underneath
is not in dispute -- an independent DOAS code reproduces Augur to within its
output resolution -- but neither of these questions can be put to a
conventional implementation without reprocessing the campaign once per
answer.

Asking them changes what the uncertainty means. For the product the instrument
reports, formed as the difference of two retrievals, leave-one-out perturbation of
the zero-air, reflectivity and etalon references gives a structural
uncertainty \fieldnum{1.4} to \fieldnum{2.5} times the sigma the fit reports for the same 60\,s
record, so the total is \fieldnum{1.7} to \fieldnum{2.7} times what is reported. The uncertainty
column delivered with the product is not that quantity at all but a gain
systematic proportional to NO$_2$, and carries none of this. On synthetic spectra with
known truth the reported sigma tracks the scatter to within about 10\,\% for
white photon noise, but omitted companion absorbers bias
the retrieval by a fixed extinction offset independent of analyte amount, and
the same truth fitted in two windows disagrees by as much as the combined
reported uncertainty. None of these enlarge the residual.

Two failures are of a different kind, because the information existed upstream
and was discarded. Wavelength shift is identifiable in one window and not in
another -- the profiled objective varies by a median \fieldnum{1.2}\,\% over $\pm4$\,px where
the other window spans five orders of magnitude -- yet both are reported as
converged, and under matched settings \fieldnum{85.5}\,\% of them terminate at a parameter
bound; a laboratory spectrum with added NO$_2$ locates the same shift to a tenth of
a pixel, two pixels from where the ambient fit converged. And a nine-hour misalignment between the reflectivity time base and the
spectra, present in twelve days of the operational product, displaced the
retrieved alkyl nitrates by \fieldnum{0.0999}\,ppb, \fieldnum{1.43} times the entire structural
budget, while moving the fitted wavelength shift by \fieldnum{0.005}\,px and changing the
residual and the reported uncertainty by two parts in a thousand.

Neither failure is expensive to prevent: the termination state, the distance
to the nearest reference in time and the provenance of each reference are all
known inside the retrieval and need only be carried to the product, which we
recommend as a reporting convention and implement in Augur. We release Augur and a synthetic benchmark with which any
DOAS implementation can measure the same quantities.
\end{abstract}
